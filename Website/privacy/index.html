1. About this policy

Mira is developed by Sriram P H. This policy covers the Mira camera and viewer apps, their companion apps, and this website. It explains how information is processed to connect the cameras and viewers you choose.

Mira does not include advertising or third-party analytics SDKs. We do not sell your personal information or use camera content for advertising. The app does not send your recordings to a developer-operated video storage service.

2. Information the app handles

  • Camera and audio content. With your permission, the camera device captures video and microphone audio for live viewing, recording, and on-device motion or sound detection. Talk-back uses the viewer’s microphone. Supported detection runs on the camera device.
  • Recordings and events. The camera stores recorded clips, event timestamps, event types, and image thumbnails. Viewers can receive footage and images when you view, replay, take a snapshot, or export.
  • Device and pairing information. Device identifiers, device and camera names, pairing secrets, authorized viewers, settings, and connection preferences allow your devices to recognize and connect to one another. Pairing information is stored locally in the system Keychain; some preferences are stored in app settings.
  • Status and connection information. Battery, charging, recording and viewer status, network addresses, and connection statistics support discovery, connection setup, quality adjustments, and alerts.

Camera images and sound may contain personal information about people in the camera’s view. You choose where cameras are placed and which devices receive access. Sharing an exported file sends it to the destination you select.

3. Connections and iCloud

Live connections. Video, audio, talk-back, playback, and transfers between a camera and its viewers use encrypted WebRTC connections. Devices connect directly where the network allows. A relay you configure may carry encrypted traffic when a direct connection is unavailable.

Cloud messages. Remote connection setup, camera status, events, and Watch fallback snapshots can use Apple’s CloudKit service. The app encrypts these message payloads with the camera’s pairing secret before storing them in CloudKit’s public database, using hashed mailbox or record identifiers. Public database storage is not the same as making the decrypted content public. Record identifiers, timing, sizes, and service metadata are not all hidden by that encryption. Apple also processes push notification routing and basic notification fields, which can include a camera name used as an alert title.

Same-account pairing. iCloud key-value storage separately syncs camera invitations, including camera names and pairing access secrets, between devices signed into the same Apple Account. This synchronization relies on Apple’s iCloud protections; it is distinct from the app-encrypted CloudKit message payloads described above.

Apple Watch. Live pictures and voice pass through the paired iPhone using WatchConnectivity. When that relay is unavailable, the Watch can request encrypted still images through iCloud. These are still-image snapshots, not cloud-stored video recordings.

4. Services that help connect your devices

Apple provides iCloud, CloudKit, push notifications, WatchConnectivity, app distribution, and TestFlight. These services process account, delivery, network, and diagnostic information under Apple’s own terms and privacy policy.

The app uses Google and Cloudflare STUN services to discover network addresses for WebRTC connections. These services can receive technical information such as your public IP address and connection requests; they are not video storage services. Their handling is described in Google’s privacy policy and Cloudflare’s privacy policy.

If you configure a TURN relay, the operator can process network metadata and any relay credentials you supply, and carry encrypted connection traffic. The operator’s own privacy and retention practices apply.

Authorized viewers receive the content and status needed for the features you use. Network and service providers may process data in countries other than yours.

5. Storage, retention, and deletion

On the camera. Recordings and event thumbnails are stored in the app’s local recording library, which the app marks as excluded from device backups. Older footage is removed as needed to enforce your storage limit. Event-based recording also discards footage outside retained event periods. You can delete clips or use “Delete all recordings” on the camera device.

On viewers and export destinations. Downloads, temporary playback files, snapshots, and exported clips may exist on a viewer or wherever you save or share them. Deleting a recording on its camera does not delete separately exported copies. Manage those copies in Photos, Files, or the service that received them.

Pairing and cloud records. Keychain entries and iCloud pairing information may persist after an app is removed. Cloud signaling messages are filtered by age, and the app attempts some message deletions, but this does not guarantee removal of all stored records. Encrypted event records, presence records, and the latest fallback snapshot may remain in CloudKit after a session ends or a camera is removed. Resetting pairing changes future access; it is not a cloud-data deletion action.

For questions or requests about information held through the app’s cloud services, contact us below. Because cloud content is encrypted and addressed by derived identifiers, we may need information from you to identify the records concerned.

6. Your privacy choices

  • Manage camera, microphone, local network, notification, and Photos permissions in your device’s settings. Permission availability depends on the platform. Photos access is requested when you choose to save an image or clip.
  • Stop camera mode or recording when you no longer want capture to continue.
  • Review devices with access on the camera. Disconnect or block viewers, or reset pairing to revoke existing pairing access.
  • Remove a camera from a viewer. The app attempts to notify the camera; an unreachable camera may keep that viewer listed until it reconnects or you remove access on the camera.
  • Delete recordings and exported copies separately. Review your iCloud settings and optional relay configuration.

Depending on your location, you may have rights to request access, correction, deletion, or restrictions on the processing of personal information we hold. Contact us to make a request. We may need to verify the request and explain any technical or legal limits.

7. Website, diagnostics, and support

This website does not use analytics scripts, advertising pixels, or tracking cookies. Fonts and visual assets are served with the website. Vercel hosts this website and may process technical request information, such as IP addresses, browser request details, and request times, to deliver and secure the site. Its handling and retention are described in Vercel’s privacy policy.

If you use TestFlight, Apple may provide the developer with beta usage information, feedback, crash reports, tester contact information, and related diagnostics under its TestFlight privacy notice. The app may also write technical diagnostic logs on your device. We do not run a separate automatic analytics or log-upload service.

If you email us or voluntarily send feedback, we receive your contact details, message, and any attachments you choose to include. We use that information to respond and resolve the request, and retain it as needed for that purpose and any applicable recordkeeping obligations.

8. Changes to this policy

We will update this page when the app’s data handling changes and revise the date above. Material changes will be described in the relevant app or release information.
